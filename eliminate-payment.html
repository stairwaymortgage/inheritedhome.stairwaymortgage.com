A reverse mortgage may allow a qualifying homeowner to remain in the home — with no monthly mortgage payment required, for as long as they live there.

Stay in the Home. Remove the Payment. Keep Your Peace.

For the right person in the right situation, a reverse mortgage — specifically a Home Equity Conversion Mortgage (HECM) — can be one of the most meaningful financial decisions available. It allows a homeowner aged 62 or older to convert home equity into accessible funds while eliminating the monthly mortgage payment entirely.

This isn't a solution for everyone. But for a surviving spouse who wants to stay in the home they've lived in for decades — without the weight of a monthly payment they can no longer comfortably afford — it can be genuinely life-changing.

How It Works

  • The homeowner must be 62 or older and the home must be their primary residence
  • No monthly mortgage payment is required — the loan balance grows over time and is repaid when the home is sold, the homeowner moves, or passes away
  • The homeowner retains full ownership and must continue paying property taxes, insurance, and basic maintenance
  • Available funds can be received as a lump sum, monthly payments, a line of credit, or a combination
  • FHA insurance ensures you can never owe more than the home is worth at the time of repayment
Who This May Help Most

A surviving spouse who is 62 or older, wishes to remain in the home, but finds the existing mortgage payment difficult or impossible to sustain on their income alone. In many of these situations, a reverse mortgage can eliminate that burden completely — providing stability, dignity, and peace of mind.

Important Considerations

  • This option reduces the equity that heirs would eventually inherit — a conversation worth having with family first
  • The homeowner must remain current on property taxes, insurance, and maintain the home as their primary residence
  • HUD-approved independent counseling is required before any reverse mortgage can be issued
  • This is generally not suitable for someone who expects to move in the near term

Required Counseling

Before a reverse mortgage can be issued, the homeowner must complete a session with a HUD-approved independent housing counselor. This is a federal consumer protection requirement — and it ensures you fully understand the terms before making any commitment.
