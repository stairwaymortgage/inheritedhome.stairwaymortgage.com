Turn One Inheritance Into Long-Term Wealth

If the inherited home is in a desirable rental market, holding it as an investment property can be a wise long-term strategy. Instead of selling a potentially appreciating asset, you keep it — collect rental income — and use that income to move forward with your own living situation.

This path turns a single inheritance into an ongoing stream of value. In the right circumstances, it becomes the foundation of a real estate portfolio that builds wealth over time.

How This Strategy Works

  • The inherited property is placed into service as a rental — long-term or short-term depending on the market
  • Rental income is documented and can often be used to help qualify for a new mortgage on your primary residence
  • DSCR loans allow the rental property to qualify based on its own income — not your personal tax returns
  • A property manager handles day-to-day operations if you prefer a hands-off approach
  • Minor repairs and preparation may be needed before the first tenant
What Is a DSCR Loan?

A DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio) loan qualifies the investment property based on the rental income it generates relative to the mortgage payment — not on the borrower's personal income or tax returns. If the rent covers the debt service, the loan often qualifies. This is especially powerful for self-employed individuals, retirees, or anyone with non-traditional income.

Before You Decide, Consider

  • What could the property realistically rent for in today's local market?
  • Would that rental income cover the mortgage, property taxes, insurance, and basic maintenance?
  • Are you prepared for landlord responsibilities — or would you budget for a property manager?
  • Is the home in good enough condition to rent without major repairs first?
  • What is your plan for your own housing — are you buying, renting, or already housed?
